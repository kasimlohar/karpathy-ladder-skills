You must find one value in a sorted list. A full scan is slow for large data. Binary search halves the range.
The rule is simple. Order tells you which half to keep. Compare the target with the middle value. Keep only the half that can hold the target.
Use three markers. Low marks the start of the range. High marks the end of the range. Mid is half way between low and high.
Check the value at mid. If it matches, stop. If the target is greater, move low up. If less, move high down.
Watch an example. Target is seven. Range is one to fifteen. Mid is eight. Eight is greater than seven, so move high down. The range is smaller.
Repeat the step. New mid is four. Four is less than seven, so move low up. The range halves again. Each step discards half of the values.
After three steps, mid is seven. It matches the target. You stop the search. Three steps replace up to fifteen checks.
Time grows slowly. Double the size adds one step only. One million items need about twenty steps. That is the power of halving. It needs sorted data.
